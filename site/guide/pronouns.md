# Pronouns & Agreement

Independent pronouns are often unnecessary because the auxiliary verb carries person via a prefix. Use full pronouns when emphasis or clarity is needed; otherwise, person is recoverable from the auxiliary. In formal writing and complex sentences, every auxiliary must carry its person prefix, even when the subject is stated explicitly.

Notes

- Plurality appears on nouns with {% tovc "pl" %} (e.g., {% tovc "river" %} → {% tovc "river+pl" %}), not on verbs.
- Full pronouns may precede the verb for emphasis in formal or contrastive contexts; otherwise, rely on verbal agreement.
- Third-person <code>a-</code> may be omitted in short, unambiguous casual speech. Retain it in formal writing and complex sentences; first- and second-person prefixes remain required.
- Every auxiliary in one shared-subject chain agrees with that subject. A separately embedded clause may have its own subject and agreement. See [auxiliary nesting and scope](verbs.html) for examples and the remaining question of temporal dependence.
