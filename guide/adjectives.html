Attributive Nouns (adjectives)

Descriptive qualities are expressed as nouns placed directly after the noun they modify. Order matters.

Truth-shards and hard truths

yeolath means half-truth or truth-shard: a truth partly understood or selectively revealed. Someone may possess only a fragment of the truth, or deliberately offer only that fragment. Context distinguishes sincere limited understanding from deliberate omission.

This is a lexicalized compound of “truth” and “shard.” The proto-root keraθa (“shard/fragment”) develops into kerath. Combining the proto-roots for truth+shard at year 0 and applying the sound-change script produces yeolath; its modern form reflects the shared sound history.

  • i-le-yeolathe — “the half-truths / truth-shards.”
  • i-le-yeole lha — “the hard truths.”

The second expression uses lha (“hardness/strength”) as an attributive noun. The compound yeolath is one lexical word; the attributive phrase keeps “truth” and “hardness” separate.
